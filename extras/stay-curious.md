# Stay curious

You can get through a hackathon by copying things that work. You get much more out of it if you find out why they
work. None of this is required reading. Pick one thing that matches what you were confused by this weekend, on the
bus home or the week after.

## How the AI you used works

- [Intro to Large Language Models](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=zjkBMFhNj_g) by Andrej Karpathy (video, 1 hour):
  what a language model is, how it's trained and where it goes wrong, with no maths needed.
- [But what is a neural network?](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=aircAruvnKk) by 3Blue1Brown (video, 20 minutes):
  the clearest picture of what is happening inside, and the start of a
  [full series](https://www.3blue1brown.com/topics/neural-networks).
- [Building effective agents](https://www.anthropic.com/engineering/building-effective-agents) (Anthropic): how
  people put models to work in real products, and why the simple designs usually win.
- [Prompting Guide](https://www.promptingguide.ai/): techniques for getting better answers, with examples you can
  try straight away.

## How your tools work

- [The Missing Semester of Your CS Education](https://missing.csail.mit.edu/) (MIT): the shell, Git, editors and
  debugging, the things courses assume you already know.
- [Oh My Git!](https://ohmygit.org/): a free game that shows what Git does to your files as you type commands.
- [Learn web development](https://web.dev/learn) (web.dev): short courses on HTML, CSS, accessibility and
  performance, written by browser engineers.
- [Build your own X](https://github.com/codecrafters-io/build-your-own-x): step-by-step guides to writing your own
  database, Git, web server or neural network. Pick the tool that felt most like magic this weekend.

## How to make something people can use

- [Laws of UX](https://lawsofux.com/): one card per principle, each explained in a paragraph. Ten minutes changes how
  you lay out a screen.
- [10 usability heuristics](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/ten-usability-heuristics/) (Nielsen Norman Group): the
  checklist designers have used for thirty years to find what's wrong with an interface.
- [The A11Y Project checklist](https://www.a11yproject.com/checklist/): how to check that people using a keyboard or
  a screen reader can use what you built.

## How to keep getting better

- [Teach yourself programming in ten years](https://norvig.com/21-days.html) by Peter Norvig: a short essay on why
  there's no shortcut, and what to do with the time.
- [Hackathon guide](https://hackathon.guide/) and the
  [MLH organizer guide](https://guide.mlh.io/): how hackathons are run, for when you want to help organize one.
  CodeJam is run by students.

## Three questions to take home

Curiosity is a habit more than a reading list. After the weekend, answer these for your own project:

1. Which part of your project could you not explain to a judge? Start there.
2. What did the AI assistant write that you accepted without reading? Read it now.
3. What would break first if a hundred people used your project at once? Find out why.
