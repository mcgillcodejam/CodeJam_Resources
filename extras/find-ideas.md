# Find an idea

Nobody has a good idea on demand. Teams that seem to have one usually started from something small and annoying in
their own week. This page gives you places to look. When you have three candidates, score them with the table in
[Pick a problem](../guides/02-pick-a-problem.md).

One rule first: **everything you submit is built from scratch after hacking starts.** Past projects and idea lists
are here to get you thinking. Copying one is against the rules, and judges have seen the popular ones many times.

## Start from your own week

Ten minutes, everyone writes alone, then read them out. Don't judge the answers yet.

- What did you do by hand last week that a computer should have done?
- What did you pay for, wait for or miss because you didn't know something in time?
- What do you keep a spreadsheet, a notes file or a group chat for?
- What does a friend, parent or grandparent find hard that you find easy?
- What do you do on your phone that you wish you didn't have to open your phone for?
- What went wrong for you this term that you could have undone, if only something had warned you?

The best answers name one person and one moment: "my roommate, every Sunday, when the laundry room is full".

## Prompts for the CodeJam 16 challenges

These are questions to start a conversation. They come from the one-line descriptions in
[Pick a problem](../guides/02-pick-a-problem.md) and are our own reading of them. The opening ceremony has the real
details, and for some challenges it is the only source.

| Challenge | Ask yourselves |
| --- | --- |
| Most Sustainable | What does someone on campus throw away, leave running or travel for that they wouldn't if they could see the cost? What would make the low-carbon choice the easy one? |
| Friday Night | What stops a group of friends from actually meeting up: deciding where, knowing who's free, getting home? What would make it easier to talk to someone new in person? |
| Undo Button | Which mistakes do people make that are hard to take back: a sent message, a missed deadline, a deleted file, a bad purchase? Can you catch it earlier or make it reversible? |
| Best Passive App | What do you check your phone for ten times a day that could tell you once, at the right moment? What should happen without you opening anything? |
| Best Beginner Hack | What is the smallest thing your team can build that works completely? Pick an idea where you'll learn one new tool, not four. |
| Fintech (Brim), Health and Supporting Vulnerable People, Best AI Solution, Best Pitch | Wait for the opening ceremony: Brim sets its own challenge, and the others are explained there. Bring your "own week" list and see which problems fit. |

## Local data that gives you an idea by itself

Open a dataset, scroll for five minutes, and ask "who would want to know this, and when?"

- [Montréal open data](https://donnees.montreal.ca/): snow removal, bike counts, permits, 311 requests, trees,
  inspections. Local data makes a demo feel real to Montréal judges.
- [BIXI open data](https://bixi.com/en/open-data/): every bike-share trip and station.
- [Statistics Canada developers](https://www.statcan.gc.ca/en/developers): national data through an API.
- [Environment Canada weather API](https://api.weather.gc.ca/): forecasts, alerts and climate data, free and open.
- [Our World in Data](https://ourworldindata.org/): clean, downloadable data on energy, health, food and more, with
  the chart already made so you can see whether there's a story.

## Lists of problems worth solving

- [UN Sustainable Development Goals](https://sdgs.un.org/goals): 17 goals with targets underneath; pick one target
  and make it small and local.
- [Y Combinator: Requests for Startups](https://www.ycombinator.com/rfs): what investors say they wish someone would
  build. Read it for the problems, then shrink one to a weekend.
- [How to get startup ideas](https://paulgraham.com/startupideas.html) by Paul Graham: a long essay with one short
  point, that good ideas come from noticing problems you have yourself.
- [App ideas](https://github.com/florinpop17/app-ideas): a list of practice projects sorted by difficulty. Useful for
  first-time teams as a warm-up or a starting shape; change it until it's yours.

## See what others have built (then close the tab)

- [Devpost project gallery](https://devpost.com/software): thousands of hackathon projects. Look at how winners
  explain their project in the first two lines, more than at what they built.
- [GitHub topic `codejam16`](https://github.com/topics/codejam16): this year's projects appear here as teams add the
  topic, so you can see everyone's work after the deadline.

Give yourselves a time limit. Twenty minutes of looking is research. Two hours is hiding from the decision.

## Make the idea smaller

When you have an idea, cut it down before you start:

1. Say it in one sentence: "**[Project]** helps **[who]** **[do what]** by **[how]**."
2. Remove every "and" from the sentence. Each one is a second project.
3. Name the one moment in the demo where it does something useful. Build that moment first.
4. Write the rest on a "next" list. You'll show that list to the judges as what you'd do with more time.
