# 5. Demo and judging

Judging is Sunday from 13:00 to 15:30 on the 4th and 5th floors of Trottier. Your slot and room are on your
CodeJam dashboard once the schedule is published.

## Follow a team: Sunday, seven minutes

The team's slot is mid-afternoon. They are outside the room five minutes early with the laptop charged, notifications
off and the backup video open in a tab.

Maya starts with a person, not with the technology: "Last month Wen missed the last bus twice. Each time it cost her
a taxi." Thirty seconds in, the judges know what the problem is and who has it. Then one sentence on what LastBus
does, and Wen starts the demo.

At minute three Maya does the part they rehearsed most. She holds out her phone and says, "Type in your own number."
A judge does, and a few seconds later his phone buzzes: time to leave. He laughs. That is the moment the panel will
remember when they compare notes, and it took the team no extra code, only the nerve to hand the demo over.

Then the wifi drops and the second demo step hangs. Tariq doesn't apologise for a minute. He says, "The network just
dropped; here's the same step recorded this morning," plays twenty seconds of the video, and carries on. They finish
at 4:50.

In the questions a judge asks what happens when a bus is late. They didn't build that. Wen says so: "We use the
schedule, not live positions. The STM publishes live data too, and that's the first thing we'd add." It is an honest
answer, and it shows they know where the edge of their project is.

## How it works

- Your team presents once, to **one panel of judges** in a room.
- **7 minutes in total: a 5-minute presentation, then 2 minutes of questions.**
- **Around minute 3, hand it over:** invite the judges to try your demo themselves, or walk them through it live.
  Judges remember what they touched.
- Every judge scores on the same rubric. The final rubric is announced at the opening; it rewards:
  - **Originality**: a real twist, not a clone
  - **Technical work**: what you built this weekend, and that it works
  - **Fit with the challenge** you're entering
  - **Design**: someone new can use it
  - **Demo**: clear, confident, on time
  Judges also note which challenge they'd put your project in.
- After the slots, judges deliberate for 30 minutes. Winners are announced at the closing ceremony at 16:30.

## A 5-minute shape that works

| Minute | What |
| --- | --- |
| 0:00 | The problem, through one real person (30 seconds) |
| 0:30 | What you built, in one sentence |
| 1:00 | Live demo of the main flow |
| 3:00 | Judges try it, or you take their lead |
| 4:15 | How it works (one diagram or one sentence per part), and what's next |
| 5:00 | Questions |

A copy-paste script is in [templates/pitch-7-minutes.md](../templates/pitch-7-minutes.md).

## Before your slot

- Rehearse twice with a timer. Cut anything that pushes you past 5 minutes.
- Have a **backup demo video** open in a tab. If the live demo breaks, say so, play the video, keep going.
- Charge the laptop, close everything else, turn notifications off, zoom the screen in.
- Be at your room 5 minutes early. Know who says what.
- In the questions, "we didn't get to that, here's how we would" is a good answer.
