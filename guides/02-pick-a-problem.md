# 2. Pick a problem

Spend the first hour here, not in code. A small idea done well beats a big idea half done.

## Follow a team: Friday, 19:00

Hacking has just started and the team has three ideas on the table. Tariq wants an AI study assistant. Chloé wants a
map of every free event in Montréal. Wen says, a little embarrassed, that she missed the last bus twice this month
and paid for a taxi both times.

They score the three with the table below. The study assistant sounds impressive, but nobody can name one person who
needs it more than the tools that already exist, and a dozen other teams will build the same thing. The events map
needs data nobody has. Wen's bus problem scores highest on every line: there is a real person (Wen), the demo is one
clear moment (your phone tells you to leave now), the STM publishes its schedules, and it fits a challenge, Best
Passive App, without any explaining.

It is also the least exciting idea of the three, and that is normal. The idea that wins the scoring is often the
smallest one. By 20:00 they have their sentence: "LastBus helps students who stay late on campus get home by texting
them when it's time to leave for the last bus." Maya writes it at the top of the README, and nobody has opened an
editor yet.

## CodeJam 16 challenges

Every project competes for Overall (1st to 3rd) and the popular vote. You can also aim at one or more challenges;
judges decide which challenge your project fits best, so make the fit obvious in your demo and README.

## Score your ideas in five minutes

Give each idea 1 to 5 on each line and add them up. Build the highest score, not the coolest-sounding one.

| Question | 1 | 5 |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Can you name **one real person** with this problem? | "People in general" | "My roommate, every week" |
| Can you show it working in **a 3-minute demo**? | Needs a week of setup | One clear flow, start to finish |
| Can your team **build the core in one day**? | New language, new framework, new hardware | Tools you already know |
| Does it **fit a challenge** clearly? | You'd have to explain the link | Judges see it in the first sentence |
| Is it **yours**? | A clone of an app everyone knows | A twist nobody else in the room will have |

## Write it down

Before anyone codes, agree on one sentence: "**[Project]** helps **[who]** **[do what]** by **[how]**." If you can't
write it, keep talking. That sentence becomes the top of your README and the first line of your pitch.
